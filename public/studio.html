MIDI keyboard

—

Plug a MIDI keyboard in, press Connect keyboard, and say yes to the permission box. Play chooses what it drives — the 808, the drum lanes, your uploaded samples, or one of the synth voices. The readout blinks with every note it receives, so you can tell it's working before you hear anything.

Chrome or Edge, on a computer. Safari has no MIDI at all, so iPhone and iPad can't do this — including Chrome on an iPhone, which is Safari underneath. And it only works on the https:// web address: opening the app on a home-network address like 192.168.1.50 will make the Connect button fail no matter what you do.

Arrangement

—

Write the song down once and it plays itself. Each block is a section — click its letter to change which variation it plays, and −/+ to make it longer or shorter. Turn Song mode on and press Play; the sections change on their own, and Download renders the whole arrangement instead of just what's on screen.

Bassline

—

Click a cell to place a note, click it again to clear it. One note per column — a bassline is one note at a time. The S button under a column slides into that note from the one before instead of restarting it. Bars are marked every four columns.

Live looper

Press Rec and play — recording starts on the nearest bar line, so being a little early or late doesn't matter. Press Stop and it snaps to a bar and starts looping immediately. Turning a loop off and back on brings it in in time with the song, not from its own beginning. Use headphones: on speakers the microphone records the backing track into your loop. If your layers drift late, nudge Timing trim.

Mixer

One strip per instrument. The slider is volume, the thin one under it is left/right (double‑click to re‑centre). M mutes, S plays only that track — solo several at once to hear just those.

Reverb & delay

One reverb and one delay, shared by everything — the rev and dly sliders on each mixer strip decide how much of that track goes into them. Sharing is the point: it's what makes parts sound like they're in the same room. The delay is locked to the tempo, so it stays in time when you change the BPM.

Export

Master it measures the finished mix — how loud it is, and whether it's bass‑heavy or harsh — then corrects the balance and brings it up to a normal listening level. It tells you exactly what it changed, and it won't touch anything that's already sitting right.

Renders the drums, the bassline and any loop that's switched on into a single audio file you can send to anyone. It's rendered rather than recorded, so it comes out clean no matter what the browser is doing — and faster than real time.

Save your work

Saves the bassline, the tempo, your sound settings and every recorded loop — to the server, not this browser, so it's still there on your phone. Click a name to load it, shift-click to delete it.

Shape the sound

The punch is a fast pitch drop into the note, set as a ratio above it rather than fixed Hz — so a low note and a high note keep the same character. Drive is what makes a sub note audible on a phone speaker: the fundamental is below what the speaker can reproduce, so you hear the harmonics instead.
