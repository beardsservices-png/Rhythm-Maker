1 · Make your first beat in 5 minutes

  • 1A template — a finished little song in a style (Trap, Boom Bap, Lo-Fi, House, R&B). Best way to start: pick one and change it.
  • 2Blank — kick, snare, clap, hats and an 808, all empty, for starting from scratch.
  • 3Your saved tracks — anything you saved, click to open it.
  • 4× closes this and carries on with whatever you were last working on.
  1. Open the Studio. Pick Trap on the start screen.
  2. Press Space (or ▶ Play). The song plays left to right across the timeline — watch the white line move and the lights blink next to each instrument as it sounds.
  3. Click the Kick name on the left. The drum machine below shows its pattern. Click some squares — you'll hear the change on the next loop.
  4. Click the 808 name. Now the piano roll shows its notes. Click in an empty spot to add a note; click a note to remove it.
  5. On the timeline, drag a block left or right to move it. Drag its end to make it longer. That's how you arrange the song.
  6. When you like it: Save (top right) to keep it, Export to download a .wav — mastered.

2 · The screen, piece by piece

  • 1Play / Stop. Space does the same from anywhere.
  • 2Loop parts / Play song. Play song plays the timeline start to finish. Loop parts just loops each instrument's current pattern — for building a beat before you arrange it. If the timeline looks greyed out, you're in Loop parts.
  • 3● Rec — record a take. See Recording.
  • 4① Song timeline — WHEN each instrument plays. Time runs left to right in bars.
  • 5② Editor — WHAT the selected instrument plays. Pick any instrument from the Editing box, or click its name in the timeline.
  • 6The keyboard — always at the bottom of the screen. Play it with the mouse, your computer keys, or a MIDI keyboard.
  • 7The knobs for whatever the keyboard is playing (808 punch, decay, drive… or tone/attack/release for other sounds).
  • 8? Manual — this page. The round ? buttons jump to the right part.
The page scrolls. The timeline is on top, the editor under it. Scroll down for the editor and mixer; the keyboard stays pinned to the bottom. The ▾ button on the keyboard hides it if you need the room.

3 · What's making that sound?

  • 1The light next to an instrument blinks every time it makes a sound. The whole row brightens too. If something sounds wrong, watch which light blinks with it.
  • 2The playhead — the white line is "now". Everything it's crossing is playing.
  • 3A block — the little marks inside it are the actual hits/notes, so you can see what it plays without opening it.
  • 4The name — click it and the editor below opens that instrument.
  • 5✎ does the same as clicking the name.
Upper vs lower. The timeline (upper) never makes sound by itself — it only says when. The sound itself is the pattern you see in the editor (lower). A kick hit is: the timeline says "Kick plays pattern A here" → pattern A in the drum machine has a square lit on that step → you hear it. In the drum machine, a row's name flashes when that drum hits.

4 · Patterns A, B, C, D — the one idea to get

Every instrument holds four patterns, named A, B, C and D. A pattern is a short loop (1, 2 or 4 bars): which drum steps hit, or which notes play.

A block on the timeline says "play pattern A for these bars". So you can have the kick on A in the verse and B in the hook, while the hats switch to C for a bridge — each instrument chooses on its own.

  • Make pattern B: in the editor, click B next to the instrument, then place hits/notes. Or use Copy to… to start B as a copy of A and change it.
  • Use pattern B somewhere: click a block on the timeline, then click B in the toolbar above the timeline.
  • In Loop parts mode, clicking a letter in the editor also makes that pattern play (it switches on the next bar).

5 · The song timeline

  • 1A section — a named stretch of the song (Intro, Hook, Verse…). Click the name to play from there. Drag its right edge to make it longer/shorter. ⋯ renames, duplicates, moves or deletes it.
  • 2Bar numbers — click any number to play the song from that bar.
  • 3A selected block (white outline). Drag it left/right to move it. Hold Alt while dragging to copy it instead.
  • 4Its edge — drag either end to stretch or shorten it.
  • 5The toolbar for the selected block: change which pattern it plays (A–D), Edit it, Copy, Paste, Duplicate, Solo in this section, Delete.
  • 6M / S — mute or solo an instrument everywhere (same as the mixer).
  • 7+ Section adds a section at the end (a copy of the last one).
  • 8+ Add instrument — piano, strings, organ, brass, flute, pads, leads, more drums, or an audio file.

6 · The drum machine

Click any drum's name on the timeline (Kick, Snare, Hi-hat…) and the drum machine opens with every drum shown at once.

  • 1Editing — pick any instrument to edit, right here.
  • 2Kit — changes every drum to a different kit (Trap 808, Boom Bap, House 909, Lo-Fi, Live, Classic).
  • 3What a click places: a normal Hit, a louder Accent, a quiet Soft ghost note, or a Roll ×2/×3/×4 (quick repeated hits inside one step — the trap hi-hat roll). Click a square that already has that kind of hit to clear it.
  • 4Swing — pushes every other sixteenth a little late. 0% is stiff and straight; 15–30% bounces.
  • 5This drum's sound — borrow a sound from any kit (a Boom Bap snare with a Trap kick).
  • 6A B C D — which of this drum's four patterns you're looking at (and, in Loop parts mode, playing).
  • 7The steps — 16 per bar. Lit = it hits. The thick lines are the beats (1, 2, 3, 4). Drag across squares to paint many at once. ×3 means a roll.
  • 8The drum's name — flashes yellow when it plays. Click it to select it.
Copy beat to… copies every drum's current pattern to another letter in one go — the quick way to make a "B" version of the whole beat. Clear lane empties just the selected drum's pattern.

7 · The piano roll (808, keys, strings, everything with notes)

  • 1Sound — change this instrument (Piano, Electric Piano, Organ, Strings, Brass, Flute, Pad, Lead, Pluck, Bell, Synth Bass, 808). The notes stay.
  • 2A B C D — which pattern you're editing.
  • 3Length — how long the pattern is before it repeats: 1, 2 or 4 bars.
  • 4New note — how long a note is when you click to add it (1/16 = one step, 1 bar = a whole bar).
  • 5Chords — when on, one click places a 3-note chord that fits the key.
  • 6A note. Drag it to move it (up/down = pitch, left/right = time). Drag its right end to lengthen it. Click it to delete it.
  • 7The keyboard down the side — the note names (C4 = middle C). Click a key to hear that note. With a Key picked, the key's notes are lit and the root is marked in amber — stay on lit rows and it'll sound right.
  • 8The grid — click an empty spot to add a note; keep holding and drag right to make it longer. Thin lines are steps, thicker ones beats, thickest bars.

The 808's slides

  • 1S above a note makes it slide (glide) up or down from the note before it, instead of hitting fresh — the classic trap 808 move. It only works when the note before ends right where this one starts.
  • 2A sliding note is drawn in teal.

8 · The keyboard at the bottom

  • 1Play — which instrument the keyboard plays. Pick Drum pads to get a pad for each drum instead.
  • 2Sound — change that instrument's sound.
  • 3Oct − / Oct + — move the keyboard lower/higher (or the ← → arrow keys).
  • 4Slide (808) — glide from note to note instead of restarting (or hold Shift).
  • 5● Write notes — while the song plays, whatever you play on the keys or pads gets written into the pattern, lined up to the grid. Turn it off when you're done.
  • 6The keys — click them, or use your computer keyboard: bottom row Z–M is the lower octave, top row Q–U the upper one (the letters are printed on the keys). For pads: A S D F…
  • 7The knobs for this sound. Move them while it plays. Reset puts them back.
  • 8▾ hides the keyboard to free up the screen.

9 · Recording

  • 1↶ ↷ undo / redo.
  • 2Play / Stop.
  • 3Click — a metronome tick on every beat.
  • 4● Rec — start recording. If the song is stopped you get a count of 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 first, then it starts on the beat. Press again to stop (or it stops itself after the length in 6).
  • 5What to record: my playing (only what you play on the keys/pads — not the beat), whole mix (everything you hear, "bounced" into one clip), or microphone (vocals, guitar… use headphones).
  • 6How long — 1, 2, 4, 8 bars, or until you stop it.
  • 7BPM — the tempo. Type a number.
  • 8Loop parts / Play song.
  • 9Save to the server (there on your phone too).
  • 10Export — download a .wav, with auto-mastering.

Every recording becomes a new audio track on the timeline — move it, stretch it, copy it like any block. A whole-mix "bounce" starts switched off so you don't hear everything twice; drag it where you want it and mute the parts it replaces.

Recording notes instead of sound: turn on ● Write notes on the keyboard, press Play, and play. The notes land in the pattern, where you can fix any that are off.

10 · Audio clips and uploads

+ Add instrument → Upload an audio file… brings in any audio file (a sample, a vocal, a loop). Click its name on the timeline to see it:

  • 1The waveform — the actual sound, with each bar marked. The red-tinted part (if any) is silence after the clip ends.
  • 2Loop length — how many bars of it to loop.
  • 3Clip gain — its volume (the mixer fader is on top of this).

Uploads and recordings are saved with the song.

11 · The mixer

Scroll down to the editor and click the Mixer tab.

  • 1Volume of that instrument.
  • 2Pan — left / right. Double-click to centre.
  • 3rev / dly — how much goes into the shared reverb (room) and delay (echo). The Reverb & delay tab sets how big the room is and how the echo repeats.
  • 4pump — makes this instrument duck down every time the kick hits and swell back: the "breathing" sound in house and EDM. Try it on pads and chords.
  • 5M mute, S solo.
  • 6Master — overall volume.

12 · Auto-mastering and exporting

Yes — it auto-masters. When you Export, the finished song goes through a mastering chain before it's saved: it listens to the whole mix, fixes the balance (too much bass, too harsh…), adds depth, glues it together and brings it up to the loudness of a released song, with a limiter so it never distorts.

  • 1Length — in Play song mode it's always the whole song; in Loop parts mode pick how many bars.
  • 2Auto-mastering style:
    Deep & warm (recommended) — fuller, heavier low end with the sub kept dead-centre so it hits, a touch of warmth (harmonics), wider stereo, gentle "glue" compression, a little air on top.
    Loud — the same, pushed harder for maximum level.
    Clean — only fixes the balance and level.
    Off — exactly as mixed.
  • 3▶ Hear it mastered — plays the mastered version right here, before you download. Press again to stop.
  • 4Download — saves the .wav. The message underneath says exactly what the mastering did (e.g. "low-end weight +2.5dB, warmth, stereo 30% wider, glue compression, level +6dB").

13 · Saving, opening, undo, autosave

  • 1Open lists your saved songs — click one to open it, × deletes it.
  • 2New from a template… brings back the start screen.
  • Save (or Ctrl+S) puts the song on the server — name it in the box at the top left first. Saved songs are there on any device.
  • Autosave: the Studio also keeps your latest work in this browser automatically, recordings included. Close the tab by mistake? Open the Studio and click Continue where you left off.
  • Undo / redo: Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z, or ↶ ↷. Works for patterns, the timeline, sounds and knobs (not mixer faders).

14 · Ask Claude

  • 1Claude answers here, and lists exactly what it changed.
  • 2Type what you want in plain words and press Enter.

Claude actually changes the song — the same way you would with the buttons, so you can undo it. Things to try:

  • "Add strings playing the chords in the hook."
  • "Make the hi-hats roll at the end of every bar in the hook."
  • "Drop the 808 for the last two bars of the verse."
  • "Give me a lo-fi beat at 80 with lots of swing."
  • "Turn the 808 drive up and make it slide more."

15 · A MIDI keyboard

Plug it in, press MIDI at the top, then Connect keyboard and allow it. Play chooses what it drives — usually "Same as the on-screen keys". Velocity, the sustain pedal and the pitch wheel all work. A drum pad controller plays the drums directly.

Needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. iPhones and iPads can't do MIDI in any browser (Apple doesn't allow it).

16 · On your phone

Rhythm Shop installs like an app. iPhone: open it in Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen. Android: tap Install the app on the home page. Everything works except MIDI on iPhone; turn the phone sideways for the Studio.

18 · When something's wrong

ProblemFix
No sound at allPress Play once (browsers won't make sound until you click something). Check the Master slider in the Mixer and your device volume. On iPhone, flip the silent switch off.
The timeline is greyed out / not following the songYou're in Loop parts. Click Play song.
I changed the drums but don't hear itYou may be editing a different letter than the one playing there. Click the block on the timeline first — that opens exactly the pattern it plays.
An instrument is silentLook for a red M (muted) or another instrument's S (solo) — Mixer tab → Clear solo. Or it has no block on the timeline there.
Mic recordings land lateChoose microphone in the Rec box; a Timing trim slider appears — nudge it.
Recording picks up the beatUse headphones — on speakers the mic hears the speakers.
